## Introduction

The Comptia Pentest+ Certification, according to the [official site](https://www.comptia.org/en/certifications/pentest/), is advertised as a hands-on preparation and examination path towards the world of professional pentesting. As a student who obtained the certification, I'm going to share some thoughts on the studying process, the expectations, the objectives and the results.
## Why Comptia

From a curricular standpoint, it's well known what certifications keep getting asked during an offensive cybersecurity hiring process: OSCP and CEH.
With much joy, some environments have also begun to consider the HTB CPTS certification. Nevertheless, it's still rare to encounter a job offer that cites the Comptia Pentest+ among the preferred requirements.

So why choosing it in the first place?

Short answer: it's budget friendly and easily obtainable, especially if you come from a cybersecurity background.
## What I disliked

I'm gonna start with the negative sides: I do not consider this as a hands-on certification. The exam revolves entirely around theory questions that you get to know studying the official and unofficial guides. There is no live lab to complete or an actual engagement to execute. It's just x questions to answer via multiple choices.
Although there's been a trend of implementing interactive sections that move away from multiple choices to employ a 'order the steps' style of assessment, it's still far from an OSCP exam.

Honestly speaking, I can't really figure out how they label 'hands-on' a theoretical exam. 

## What I liked

Established that we're talking about a theoretical, entry-level exam, a positive point is that it holds the hand for a newcomer in cybersecurity. As a structured prosecution along the cores of exercising a cybersecurity profession, you can study the theory-only sections in order to pass the exam, and get your head around the practical sections that need further diving in.
## Outcome

Having experienced the [HTB Academy](https://academy.hackthebox.com/preview/certifications/htb-certified-penetration-testing-specialist/) approach towards a job path, I can only suggest attending the course they offer. The issue is that completing the academy requires a lot of time, even for an experienced cybersecurity practitioner.

Comptia Pentest+ is a soft spot between resources required and return of investment. It will demonstrate that you spent a solid amount of time understanding the basics of offensive cybersecurity and add a line in you CV.

Also, if you prefer a theoretical approach, this is the way to go.

Finally, having enough time, I would still suggest aiming for a different, truly practical Certification.
