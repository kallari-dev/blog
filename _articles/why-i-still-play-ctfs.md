Every so often someone points out that capture-the-flag challenges look nothing like real work. They're right, and that is the point.

> A good challenge removes everything except the one idea it wants to teach you.

Real engagements are noisy. There are scoping calls, flaky environments and reports to write. A CTF strips all of that away and leaves a single, sharp problem: a strange binary, an odd cipher, a parser that trusts too much.

## What carries over

- **Reading unfamiliar code fast.** Challenges are written by people who want to hide something.
- **Knowing when to stop.** A clock teaches you to drop a dead end.
- **Team habits.** Splitting work, writing notes others can follow, handing over cleanly.

## What doesn't

Guessing. In a competition a hunch can pay off. In a report, every claim needs evidence.
